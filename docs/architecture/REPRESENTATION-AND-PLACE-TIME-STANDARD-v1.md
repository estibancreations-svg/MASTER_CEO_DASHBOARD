# Representation and place-time standard v1
Effective: 2026-10-01, America/Chicago. Authorized by the director in the production-test conversation.

## Default across connected creative systems
Do not silently default unspecified people to white or thin. Include Black people and fat people as ordinary protagonists, leaders, and supporting characters. Make casting intentional and record the approved appearance. Fat is a valid body description, not a defect or a joke. Do not attach stereotypes to body size or identity.
Keep race, ethnicity, nationality, culture, language, and accent separate; do not infer one from another. Existing approved characters retain their canon.

## Current production lock
The Boy and the Red Balloon: a Black African American boy, age seven, dark brown skin, tightly coiled black hair, round full cheeks, fat body with natural age-appropriate proportions, yellow raincoat, navy trousers, black rain boots, one red balloon on a white string.
The earlier reference and its derived boards are superseded. The corrected appearance requires a NEW versioned 32-view master, Character Detail Specifications Board, and A Cast Board. Preserve skin tone, hair texture, facial geometry, body size, and wardrobe across angles and motion; reject lightening, slimming, or identity drift. Reference actual approved asset IDs in generation requests and retain provider task IDs.
Follow [Character Identity Board System v1](https://github.com/estibancreations-svg/VisionWeaver/blob/main/standards/character-board-system-v1.md).
Label final film visibly AI-CREATED.

## Prompt instruction
Use this instruction when drafting new people or avatars:
"Follow approved character references. If casting is unspecified, make deliberate inclusive choices rather than a white/thin default. Include Black and fat people in normal, dignified roles. Preserve approved skin tone, hair texture, facial features and body size. Do not infer culture or accent from appearance."

## Place and time accuracy
Record scene date (or historical period), local time, timezone, geographic location and landmark identity. Verify the landmark existed then and depict its period-appropriate buildings, grounds, materials, signage, clothing, transport, weather and light. Keep evidence URLs, source dates, supported facts, and uncertainty alongside each location reference.
A present-day photograph is not evidence of historical appearance. Reject anachronisms; an unresolved historical reconstruction must be flagged before render approval. Never depict Millennium Park as existing in a 1980 Chicago scene.
Examples for later research: Cloud Gate/Chicago, Louvre/France, Taj Mahal/India. These are research targets, not verified location packs.
Global avatar states and location-pack planning begins after the current end-to-end production test.

Confirm approved boards appear in the real generation payload, review resulting stills and motion for representation consistency, test durable reload, and retain the assembled film and AI label evidence.
